## The job in sixty seconds

A story model records people, events, states, contexts, order, and the words that support each
claim. A recall graph records a person's retelling in its own sequence. The aligner compares the
two and returns a probability row for every recall unit.

Each row answers a concrete question: how much of this recall unit points to the story, where in the
story does it point, and how much belongs to an association, commentary, or another named external
state? Summary measures report the evidence that contributed to them. An unsupported measure appears
as unavailable.

The checked-in example follows this route:

| Step | Input | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Index the story | *The War of the Ghosts* | sentences, tokens, and exact character spans |
| 2. Read the reviewed model | the indexed story | 71 situations, 14 entities, 12 contexts, and typed relations |
| 3. Represent the recall | a 78-character retelling | two checked recall units with exact transcript spans |
| 4. Align the two | sparse lexical candidates | two posterior rows over story nodes and external states |
| 5. Summarize the result | the posterior and source model | coverage, fidelity, chronology, compression, and support |

The result stays conditional on the chosen recall segmentation and proposition analysis. A research
product records the segmentation policy or receipt and retains plausible alternatives.

## One sentence, two claims

*The War of the Ghosts* contains this sentence:

> One of the young men said: “I have no arrows.”

The reviewed model records the act of speaking in the narrated world. It records “I have no arrows”
inside the young man's speech, with negative polarity on `have`. Both claims cite the same sentence.

If a later recall says “He had no arrows,” an analysis can detect that the retelling presents the
character's words as a story fact. The context field makes that comparison possible.

## A real alignment result

The complete example uses this recall:

> One of the men with him got hit by an arrow and they lifted him into the boat.

The baseline segmenter produces two units. The free lexical matcher generates 86 sparse candidates,
and `GraphHsmm` assigns source and external mass to each unit. The first unit receives 46.08% source
mass. The second receives 2.09%.

The recall is a faithful paraphrase, while word overlap recovers little of it. That poor result
shows the exact API and output shape. Recall accuracy requires separate calibration evidence.

## What works today

The repository contains public Scala APIs for exact text indexing, proposition charts, evidence-backed
proposal resolution, document identity, story and recall graphs, sparse alignment, support-aware
summaries, interview scoring, feature tracks, deterministic embeddings, structural graph distance,
and canonical JSON.

The code is pre-release and runs from a source checkout. The complete *War of the Ghosts* model is a
researcher-reviewed fixture. Automatic construction of an equally complete model from arbitrary
prose remains programme work.
