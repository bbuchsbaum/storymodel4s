## One sentence, two records

*The War of the Ghosts* contains this sentence:

> One of the young men said: “I have no arrows.”

The story fixture creates two records from it:

| Record | Context | Predicate | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ym1-says-no-arrows` | narrated world | `say` | a young man says that he has no arrows |
| `lack-arrows` | the young man's speech | `have`, negative | the young man has no arrows |

The first record describes an act that occurs in the story: the young man speaks. The second records
the content of his speech. Both records cite the same sentence, numbered 14 for a reader and 13 by
the fixture's zero-based index.

This matters when a person recalls the story. A recall such as “He had no arrows” presents the
reported claim as a fact of the story. Because the story model records the two contexts separately,
an analysis can identify that change.

## A complete comparison

The public example starts with the reviewed story model and this recall:

> One of the men with him got hit by an arrow and they lifted him into the boat.

It divides the recall into two units, generates 86 candidate matches, runs `GraphHsmm`, and computes
a `RecallSignature`. The free lexical baseline assigns 46.08% source mass to the first unit and
2.09% to the second. It assigns the remaining mass to named external states. Importance-weighted
coverage is unavailable because the run has no conditioning weight for that estimate.

Those are real results from the checked-in program. They also show the limit of word overlap: the
recall is a faithful paraphrase, while the lexical baseline treats most of it as external material.
The guide prints the complete program and output and explains each result.

## What the source library provides

The current repository provides public Scala APIs for:

- indexing source and recall text by sentence, token, and exact span;
- representing claims, propositions, entities, situations, contexts, event order, and causal links;
- reading and writing PENMAN, checking AMR graphs, and converting them to the library's proposition model;
- recording parser or agent proposals, critic findings, typed patches, and deterministic resolution outcomes;
- building document mention graphs, resolving exact coreference, and projecting mentions into narrative nodes;
- representing recall units and their order;
- aligning recall units with source nodes and named external states;
- computing research summaries together with their support and missingness;
- scoring the Autobiographical Interview;
- generating deterministic embedding features and authorizing sanitized remote requests; and
- saving canonical JSON and compiling renderer-neutral views.

The code is pre-release and currently runs from a source checkout. The reviewed story fixture is
hand-authored. Automatic construction of a complete story model from arbitrary prose remains
programme work.
