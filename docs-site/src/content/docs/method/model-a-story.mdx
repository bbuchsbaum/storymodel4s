A `StoryModel` joins five parts of a story:

| Field | What you can inspect |
| --- | --- |
| `source` | the canonical text, title, language, and checksums |
| `atlas` | paragraphs, sentences, tokens, and exact spans |
| `graph` | entities, situations, contexts, and typed relations |
| `hierarchy` | the scenes and episodes that contain narrative nodes |
| `trajectory` | the order in which the telling presents those nodes |

The model also exposes its claims, feature references, descriptors, hypotheses, sensory profiles,
and optional build receipt. This example reads the public *War of the Ghosts* fixture and prints a
small part of it.

## What was computed

The fixture contains 50 sentences and 517 tokens. Its narrative graph contains 14 entities, 71
situations, and 12 contexts. The relation counts come from the graph itself: 52 temporal links,
9 causal links, and 3 goal links. `model.claims` gathers 475 claim records from the graph, hierarchy,
trajectory, descriptors, and hypotheses.

The last two records explain why contexts matter:

<StoryContextFigure />

1. `ym1-says-no-arrows` is a positive `say` event in `NarratedWorld`.
2. `lack-arrows` is a negative `have` state in the young man's `Speech` context.

Both records cite sentence 14: `One of the young men said: "I have no arrows."` The sentence supports
the act of speaking and the content of the speech. The context field tells a program which claim the
narrator presents directly and which claim belongs to the character's words.

## What this example leaves open

`WarOfTheGhostsModel.model` loads a researcher-reviewed fixture. It demonstrates the representation
after modeling has taken place. Automatic construction of this complete model from raw prose is
still under development.
