This fixture tests a specific modeling decision: where to place a reported injury and how to record
an uncertain cause of death.

## The difficult passage

In Charles Cultee's *The War of the Ghosts*, warriors say that a young man has been shot. The man
does not feel sick, later concludes that his companions are ghosts, returns home, and dies. The
text does not settle one clean causal account.

The fixture places “he has been shot” in the warriors' speech context. It records the later death
and leaves the cause open among explicit hypotheses.

## Three claims, three locations

| Content | Where it lives | What the model may say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The warriors say the man was shot | A speech context | **Reported** content |
| The man was injured in the narrated world | The root context | An open **hypothesis**, with alternatives retained |
| The man does not feel sick | The narrated world | An explicit, **negated** state |

The source states that the man dies. It leaves the cause unsettled. The model therefore keeps
competing causal hypotheses and creates no accepted wound-to-death edge.

## How the fixture is checked

The fixture carries 12 plain-language expectations. Each expectation names the phenomenon, the
relevant source sentences, a statement a researcher can read, and mechanical checks over the
model. The acceptance suite fails if any expectation fails.

```scala
val failures = WarOfTheGhostsExpectations.evaluate(WarOfTheGhostsModel.model)

assertEquals(
  failures.map((expectation, check, why) =>
    s"${expectation.phenomenon} / $check: $why"
  ),
  Vector.empty
)
assertEquals(WarOfTheGhostsExpectations.all.size, 12)
```

The suite also changes the model in ways that should fail and verifies that the checks catch those
changes. This shows that the assertions distinguish the intended model from specific faulty ones.

## Scope

This test covers one difficult, hand-authored story model. Accuracy on unseen stories, recovery from
raw text, embedding calibration, and production defaults each require their own evidence.

The story text comes from the public-domain 1901 US Government publication recorded by Franz Boas
and narrated by Charles Cultee. The project uses it as a narrative acceptance fixture. The AMR
conformance tests use published guideline examples elsewhere in the repository.
