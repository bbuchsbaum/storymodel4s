The first useful success is not a leaderboard number. It is a distinction that the representation
can preserve and a plausible wrong model cannot.

## The difficult passage

In Charles Cultee's *The War of the Ghosts*, warriors say that a young man has been shot. The man
does not feel sick, later concludes that his companions are ghosts, returns home, and dies. The
text does not settle one clean causal account.

A convenient model might turn “he has been shot” into an objective injury and then make the wound
the cause of death. The acceptance fixture refuses both promotions.

## Three claims, three locations

| Content | Where it lives | What the model may say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The warriors say the man was shot | A speech context | **Reported** content |
| The man was injured in the narrated world | The root context | An open **hypothesis**, with alternatives retained |
| The man does not feel sick | The narrated world | An explicit, **negated** state |

The later death is explicit. Its cause is not. Competing causal hypotheses may remain in the
model, but no explicit or entailed wound-to-death edge is accepted.

## What makes this a test rather than an illustration

The fixture carries 12 plain-language expectations. Each expectation names the phenomenon, the
relevant source sentences, a statement a researcher can read, and mechanical checks over the
model. The acceptance suite fails if any expectation fails.

```scala
val failures = WarOfTheGhostsExpectations.evaluate(WarOfTheGhostsModel.model)

assertEquals(
  failures.map((expectation, check, why) =>
    s"${expectation.phenomenon} / $check: $why"
  ),
  Vector.empty
)
assertEquals(WarOfTheGhostsExpectations.all.size, 12)
```

The suite also constructs adversarial mutants for prohibited failures. That is important: a test
that only recognizes the intended fixture may be checking presence, not discrimination.

## What this does—and does not—show

It shows that the current types, fixture, evaluator, and tests can preserve this epistemic
distinction on one difficult, hand-authored story model. It does not estimate accuracy on unseen
stories. It does not show that a provider can recover the same model from raw text. It does not
calibrate an embedding or select production defaults.

The story text in the fixture is the public-domain 1901 US Government publication recorded by
Franz Boas and narrated by Charles Cultee. The model is explicitly a narrative acceptance fixture,
not hand-authored AMR gold.
