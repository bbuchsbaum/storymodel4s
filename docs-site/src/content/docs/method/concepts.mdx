storymodel4s records a story, a recall, and a proposed mapping between them. Its data model gives
separate fields to facts that researchers may need to compare independently.

## Surface evidence is the anchor

Source and transcript text have stable character offsets. An explicit claim cites the exact source
spans that support it. An inferred claim cites the claims from which it was derived. A researcher
can therefore trace a result back through the model to the text.

Offsets use UTF-16 so they agree with common text and browser APIs.

## Context records who presents a claim

Every situation names a context. `NarratedWorld` holds the narrator's account. `Speech`, `Belief`,
and `Intention` frames hold content attributed to a speaker, believer, or agent.

For example, the sentence “the warriors said he was shot” creates a speaking event in
`NarratedWorld` and an injury claim in a `Speech` frame. The model records the report without
treating the quoted injury as a separate claim by the narrator.

## The model records three orders

- **Discourse order**: where content appears in the telling.
- **Story-world time**: when represented events occur.
- **Recall time**: the order in which a person produces recall units.

A story may describe events out of chronological order. A person may also jump backward while
recalling it. Separate coordinates let an analysis measure either kind of reordering.

## A recall can point to the story or beyond it

For each recall unit, the aligner distributes probability mass over source nodes and named external
categories such as association or task commentary. This representation covers omissions, merged
events, split events, reordering, elaboration, and added material. The output reports external mass
by category.

## Fidelity has several parts

Alignment and fidelity answer different questions. A recall can point to the correct event while
giving the wrong actor or reversing two roles. The model assesses actor, object, action, location,
context, polarity, modality, and role structure separately, while retaining the event anchor.

## Every estimate reports its support

Conditional estimates carry their supporting mass or numerator and denominator. When an estimate
has no supported cases, the result is `Missing` and the rendered output says `n/a`. A zero remains
available for cases where the measured value really is zero.

## Embeddings provide candidate features

The portable embedding contract includes deterministic hashed n-gram and TF-IDF baselines. A local
ONNX sentence-encoder adapter and a structural graph-kernel adapter also exist in JVM-only modules.
These vectors supply feature views for candidate generation and declared costs. Content hashes,
typed graph records, evidence, and validation rules determine model identity and accepted claims.
