`RecallSignature.compute` describes several parts of one alignment. It reports coverage, fidelity,
specificity, compression, chronology, causal preservation, semantic flow, backward movement,
distortion, and external mass. Conditional estimates include the mass that supports them.

This program repeats the alignment from the previous guide and prints the full summary.

## Estimates and support

<RecallSummaryFigure />

The long estimate bar and thin support line answer separate questions. The estimate describes the
aligned portion of the recall. Support describes how much evidence contributed to that estimate.

### Fidelity

Fidelity is `0.5000`. Its support is `1.0000` because every bit of the run's `0.4817` source mass
could be assessed for fidelity. That support is conditional on source placement. The alignment guide
showed that source mass accounts for only about one quarter of the two recall rows, so the fidelity
estimate describes a small part of the whole recall.

### Chronology

Discourse chronology is `0.2686`, with support `0.0082`. Story-world chronology is `0.1997`, with
support `0.0047`. Less than one percent of the transition mass could be ordered on either clock.
These values therefore describe a very small part of the inferred route.

The two clocks answer different questions. Discourse chronology follows the order of presentation in
the story. Story-world chronology follows the order in which represented events occur.

### Causal preservation

Causal preservation is `n/a`, with support `0.0000/9.0000`. The story model contains nine causal
links, and this alignment supplies no pair of recalled endpoints that the estimator can assess. The
figure uses a hatched row and the label `n/a`; it does not draw a zero-valued estimate.

### External mass

Attributed external mass is `0.7591`. Ranked mass is `1.0000`, so every unit of external mass
received a category. The poor lexical match discussed in the alignment guide drives much of this
result.

## Interpret the version too

The first line, `recall-signature/v2`, identifies the estimand definitions used by this run. A later
version may change a formula or conditioning population. Results with different estimand versions
require a definition-level comparison before a numerical comparison.
