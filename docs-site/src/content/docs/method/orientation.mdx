Suppose a person hears a story and later recalls it. “How accurate was the recall?” sounds like one
question. In practice it hides several:

- Which parts of the story were recalled at all?
- Was an event preserved but one participant reversed?
- Did the person compress several moments into a gist?
- Did the recall move backward, revisit a scene, or add an association?
- Was a statement asserted by the narrator, reported by a character, or merely believed?

storymodel4s provides typed structures for keeping those questions apart. It is an experimental
library for building and analyzing explicit story and recall models.

## The path through a model

1. **Surface evidence** gives every source and recall passage stable offsets.
2. **Narrative structure** represents entities, situations, contexts, relations, hierarchy, and
   story-world order.
3. **Recall structure** represents idea units, discourse function, expressed uncertainty, and
   recall-side relations.
4. **Alignment** assigns partial mass from each recall unit to source nodes and explicit external
   states. It also records transition flow between successive units.
5. **Derived summaries** describe coverage, fidelity facets, localizability, compression,
   chronology deformation, and intrusion mass—with support carried alongside results.

Each stage preserves its evidence, alternatives, and support. A researcher can inspect how a later
summary was obtained.

## Structures and numerical features

Typed records carry propositions, narrative contexts, participant roles, temporal and causal
relations, and source evidence. Numerical features propose possible matches and contribute declared
costs. Content hashes and typed records provide identity; validation and evidence support claims.

Two passages can have similar vectors while assigning an action to different people or using
opposite polarity. The aligner can connect such a recall to the relevant event and record the
disagreement in its fidelity fields.

## Where the method stops today

Today the repository validates and analyzes story and recall models. It also compiles accepted
proposal evidence through a deterministic interface. Providers for unattended construction of a
complete model from arbitrary prose are still under development.

The guides therefore begin with a researcher-reviewed *War of the Ghosts* fixture. It tests the
representation after modeling. Automatic acquisition needs a separate evaluation.
