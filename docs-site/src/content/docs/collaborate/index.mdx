This route is intentionally separate from the learning path. You do not need the project's
internal operating record to evaluate the method or use the source library.

## Begin with a claim small enough to test

A useful contribution names:

1. the behavior, invariant, or scientific distinction it changes;
2. the exact modules and paths in scope;
3. the evidence that would distinguish success from a plausible wrong answer;
4. the platform and command that exercise that evidence;
5. what remains unsupported after the change.

That shape works for code, fixtures, docs, and design proposals. It also makes review less about
confidence and more about whether the proposed court can fail.

## Respect the architecture before extending it

storymodel4s keeps provider code out of portable modules, makes contexts mandatory, carries support
with estimates, and prevents embeddings from adjudicating narrative truth. A new dependency,
module, or public vocabulary should begin as an architectural discussion, not appear incidentally
inside an implementation diff.

[Read the exact decision records →](/reference/decisions/)

## Treat fixtures as scientific material

Story and participant text need a recorded attribution and redistribution basis. The *War of the
Ghosts* fixture is attributed to narrator Charles Cultee and uses the public-domain English
translation in the 1901 US Government publication recorded by Franz Boas. A convenient online copy
or a pseudonymized transcript does not by itself establish permission.

## Report evidence at the right strength

- Source inspection can confirm a type or schema fact.
- A local test run is a bounded observation.
- Performance needs a named dataset, configuration, metric, baseline, and exact artifact.
- A release or deployment needs evidence from the released or deployed object.

Do not upgrade a result in prose. If the evidence is local or partial, label it that way and make
the next stronger check obvious.

## Keep the public story readable

Documentation should answer a reader's question before exposing build mechanics. Public pages cite
exact primary sources. Internal plans, review transcripts, and governance history stay on the
project board.
